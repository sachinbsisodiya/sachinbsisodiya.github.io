From Agronomy to Incentives: Moral Hazard, Diversion, and Sequential Screening in India’s Licit Opium Cultivation

When a regulator procures from agents who can divert output to illicit markets, standard monitoring tools may fail to separate low effort from deliberate diversion. This problem is acute in India, the world’s largest legal opium producer, supplying alkaloids for the global pharmaceutical market. A longstanding policy question, one that a joint U.S.–India field initiative could not resolve through agronomic measurement, is whether low opium yields reflect poor farming or deliberate diversion to illicit markets. India’s Central Bureau of Narcotics (CBN) licenses farmers under two regimes: the lucrative traditional Gum method, under which farmers handle raw opium gum, creating an opportunity for diversion, and the low-return Concentrate of Poppy Straw (CPS) method, which eliminates diversion but induces severe moral hazard. The CBN offered CPS farmers who achieved ≥900 kg/ha the opportunity to switch to Gum. Using farmer-level panel data spanning 472,805 farmer-year observations across 19 District Opium Officer’s Offices, I document three results. First, the switching threshold resolved moral hazard: within-farmer CPS yields increased by 203 kg/ha (27 percent), with broad-based gains across the yield distribution; the effect replicates independently in a second cohort (251 kg/ha) when the threshold was reaffirmed, consistent with a credibility mechanism and ruling out coincident shocks. Second, among 18,732 switchers, systematic underperformance in morphine and gum yield emerged relative to always-Gum farmers. Third, exploiting spatial variation in monitoring intensity, within-state distance to District Opium Officer’s Offices, I find that the switcher penalty does not vary with enforcement proximity, ruling out a simple model of remote-village diversion but consistent with diversion responding to the overall enforcement regime. The switching mechanism thus functions as a sequential screen, resolving moral hazard while revealing the very diversion that agronomic measurement could not detect.

US-China Trade War: Impact on potential trade of their FTA partners - Book Chapter with Swati Singh
Book: The China-US Trade War and South Asian Economies, Routledge Frontiers of Political Economy
Full Text PDF

This chapter explores the impact of the US–China trade war on their respective FTA partners. Covering three crucial sectors – automobiles, electrical machinery, and iron and steel – the chapter tries to understand whether the trade war has benefited the FTA partners of both China and the USA. The study considers two time periods: between 2013 and 2017 as the pre-tariff escalation period and 2018 as the post-tariff escalation period. The chapter comments that the automobile sector of the US has a comparative advantage in the world and the export potential of this sector is more promising than the two other selected sectors. On the other hand, China is the hub of automobile parts, known for electrical machinery and the iron and steel sector. Therefore, China’s export potential in the three selected sectors is very significant and promising for its FTA partners.

Roadblocks towards the Right to Education: Experiences from Delhi - with Vijay Prakash
Economic and Political Weekly, Volume 54, Issue No 50
Full Text PDF

This fieldwork experiment examines barriers to implementing India’s Right to Education (RTE) Act in Delhi’s slums, focusing on Section 12(1)(c), which reserves 25% of private school seats for economically disadvantaged children. Through surveys in three slum communities, we found systemic gaps: low awareness of RTE provisions, bureaucratic delays, and schools charging hidden fees despite tuition reimbursements. While learning outcomes for RTE beneficiaries were stronger than in government schools, disparities persisted due to unequal access to materials and coaching. The paper highlights policy failures in accountability and recommends grassroots awareness campaigns, stricter monitoring, and equitable resource distribution to uphold RTE’s promise.

Can Informational Nudges Increase Perception of Risk and Disapproval of Prescription Pain-reliever Misuse? - with Rene Saran — R & R at Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Economics

We conducted a lab experiment involving 610 undergraduate students at the University of Cincinnati to evaluate the impact of informational nudges on risk perception and disapproval of prescription pain-reliever misuse (PPM). The nudges, derived from NSDUH data, highlighted harmful outcomes associated with PPM in youth and were presented in four variations based on message framing (positive vs. negative) and minimal engagement (inclusion vs. exclusion of a comprehension check). A control group received no nudges. We find that positively framed messages combined with minimal engagement significantly enhanced both the perception of great risk and strong disapproval of regular (once or twice every month) misuse compared to the control group while negatively framed messages with minimal engagement only significantly increased strong disapproval for regular misuse. These effects were strongly influenced by gender and individual behavioral traits. However, we find no significant effects of the informational nudges on risk perception and disapproval for occasional (once or twice every in a lifetime or once or twice every year) and habitual (once or twice every week) misuse frequencies. The findings underscore the importance of framing and engagement as well as tailored messaging strategies in shaping young adults' attitudes toward PPM.

When Insurance Becomes a Loophole: Plough-Back Exemptions and Village Productivity in India’s Opium Licensing System - with Olivier Parent

India licenses every opium cultivator for one year at a time and renews the licence only if the gum yield tendered to the government reaches a minimum qualifying yield (MQY). One exemption has persisted throughout: a cultivator who uproots, or ploughs back, the entire crop under official supervision is not assessed against the MQY and may be licensed again. The exemption is meant to protect cultivators against crop loss from natural calamities, but a national audit found uprooting on 60–87% of measured area in Uttar Pradesh, with no calamity on record, and described it as a loophole. We ask whether villages whose licensed area is held by such uproot retainers are less efficient. Using administrative records on every licensed cultivator in Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh from 1995–96 to 2018–19, aggregated to 60,350 village-years in 4,893 villages, we estimate a Bayesian spatial Durbin stochastic frontier with village and year fixed effects in which village inefficiency is scaled by the share of licensed area held by uproot retainers, controlling for licensee experience, marginal qualifiers and temperature. In every specification, a 10 percentage point higher uproot-retainer share raises expected inefficiency by about 1.3–1.6% of output, and uprooting by neighboring villages raises it further. The plough-back exemption therefore carries an efficiency cost that falls on the following season and spreads to neighbors, which argues for verifying uprooting against recorded damage and for limiting repeated exemptions.

Age of Initiating Prescription Pain-Reliever Misuse and Post-misuse Conditions - with Rene Saran and Nayoung Lee — Under review at the Journal of Drug Issues

This paper analyzes the association between individuals’ age of initiating prescription pain-reliever misuse and their socioeconomic and health conditions post-misuse or after an initial misuse episode (26 years and older). We find that individuals who initiate prescription pain-reliever misuse in pre-adulthood compared to adulthood have statistically significantly higher odds of living in poverty, being on a government assistance program, having lower levels of education, and experiencing lower overall post-misuse health conditions. Moreover, importantly, delaying initiation of misuse by one year in pre-adulthood significantly lowers the odds of having such adverse socioeconomic and health outcomes after an initial misuse episode, now also including the odds of having illicit drug dependence. However, such a delay in adulthood does not lead to any statistically significant reduction in the odds.

State Gabapentin Regulations and Prescribing Intensity: A Staggered Difference-in-Differences Approach - with Rene Saran

Gabapentin prescriptions in the United States nearly doubled between 2012 and 2020, driven largely by off-label use for chronic pain , often as a substitute for opioids. While several states responded by adding gabapentin to their Prescription Drug Monitoring Programs (PDMPs) or classifying it as a Schedule V controlled substance, the majority of states imposed no gabapentin-specific restrictions. Existing evidence on the effects of these regulations relies on Medicare claims data, which cannot observe clinical prescribing behavior , the diagnoses motivating prescriptions, dosing intensity, treatment duration, or concurrent opioid co-prescribing. This paper uses electronic health record (EHR) data from the NeuroBlu database to examine provider-level gabapentin prescribing patterns across states with and without monitoring restrictions. Exploiting the staggered adoption of PDMP and Schedule V policies between 2018 and 2025, we employ a difference-in-differences design with staggered treatment timing to estimate the causal effect of gabapentin restrictions on prescribing intensity, off-label use, high-dose prescribing, and opioid co-prescribing. We then characterize overprescribing in states that remained unregulated throughout the study period: specifically, whether providers in these states prescribed at higher doses, for longer durations, for a broader range of off-label indications, and with greater opioid co-prescribing relative to providers in regulated states. Preliminary results indicate that the absence of monitoring is associated with substantially higher rates of high-dose gabapentin prescribing and more frequent co-prescribing with opioids , patterns consistent with regulatory moral hazard among providers. These findings suggest that the patchwork nature of state-level gabapentin regulation creates prescribing environments in which overprescribing persists unchecked, and that extending PDMP coverage or scheduling requirements to additional states could meaningfully reduce gabapentin misuse risk.
